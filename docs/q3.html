Question 3

Station net inflow and outflow

Question of interest

Which stations have net inflow or outflow, and how does the pattern differ between ordinary weekdays and weekends?

For each station: daily net inflow = average arrivals per day − average departures per day. Departures are counted by start time and arrivals by end time. Station IDs that differ only by a trailing zero (same name, within 2 m) are combined, as in the notebook. Both maps use the same extent and the same symbol-area scale.

D3 visualization

Hover a symbol for its value. Click a station on either map to highlight it on both maps and compare its weekday and weekend figures. Click empty space to clear the selection.

What the visualization shows

  
Most stations are close to balanced (the median imbalance is about 2 bikes per day), but a few dozen stations gain or lose more than 10 bikes per day: 38 on weekdays and 46 on weekends.

  
By area, the two maps differ clearly. On weekdays the orange triangles are concentrated in Midtown (roughly 14th–59th St), which loses about 295 bikes per day in total, while Downtown below 14th St gains about 200 and Harlem / Upper Manhattan above 96th St gains about 60 (58% of its stations have net inflow). On weekends Midtown turns slightly positive (+86), and the area north of 59th St becomes a net source: the Upper East / West Side loses about 35 bikes per day and Harlem and the north about 77, with only 37% of stations north of 96th St showing net inflow. Downtown remains the main sink (+181). Weekday riding drains Midtown, while weekend riding moves bikes from uptown toward downtown.

  
Individual stations on weekdays. The largest net-outflow stations, where more bikes leave than arrive, are almost all in Midtown East and Murray Hill: E 39 St & Lexington Ave (−28.6 per day), E 33 St & Park Ave (−23.0), E 47 St & 2 Ave (−21.4), E 35 St & Madison Ave (−17.1) and E 51 St & 2 Ave (−15.8). The largest net-inflow stations are in the Lower East Side and East Village: Norfolk St & Broome St (+28.1), Kenmare St & Elizabeth St (+23.4), Stanton St & Chrystie St (+22.5) and St Marks Pl & 2 Ave (+15.1). On weekdays, many bikes leave the east-side Midtown office area and end up downtown.

  
Individual stations on weekends. Outflow moves into the East Village (E 2 St & Ave B, Cooper Square, St Marks Pl & 1 Ave, E 1 St & 1 Ave at −16 to −20), next to stations that had net inflow on weekdays. Inflow appears at Hudson Yards / the far West Side (W 33 St & 10 Ave, +25.1) and in Tribeca and Lower Manhattan (Warren St & W Broadway, Centre St & Chambers St, about +15). 88 stations change direction between weekdays and weekends with an imbalance of more than 5 bikes per day on at least one day type. For example, FDR Drive & E 35 St goes from +4.7 on weekdays to −23.2 on weekends, and 8 Ave & W 31 St from +12.2 to −21.0. Rebalancing that is planned around the weekday pattern would send bikes to the wrong places on weekends.

  
Reading the view. This is recorded trip flow, not dock inventory: Citi Bike's own rebalancing trucks and bikes, and valet service, are not in the trip data. A station that shows a large net inflow every day (for example Norfolk St & Broome St on both day types) must be emptied by operations. The data includes only trips that started in September, so arrivals from trips that started in August are missing.
