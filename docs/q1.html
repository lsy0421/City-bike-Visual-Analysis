Hourly departure patterns

Question of interest

How do average hourly departures differ between ordinary weekdays and weekends?

Trips that start and end in Manhattan, September 2026 (manhattan-data). Each hour's trip count is divided by the number of days of that type (21 ordinary weekdays, Labor Day excluded; 8 weekend days), so the two lines are directly comparable even though there are many more weekdays.

What the visualization shows

  
Weekdays have two sharp peaks. Departures climb from fewer than 900 trips per day at 05:00 to about 8,545 at 08:00, drop to roughly 4,100 at 10:00–11:00, and then rise to the day's highest point of about 11,409 at 17:00 (10,012 at 18:00). The evening peak is higher than the morning one, probably because after-work trips also include errands and social trips, not just the ride home.

  
Weekends have no commuting peaks. Riding builds slowly through the morning (about 1,500 trips at 08:00, less than a fifth of the weekday level) and then stays on a broad plateau of about 4,800–5,440 trips per hour from 12:00 to 18:00, highest at 15:00. Between 11:00 and 14:00 the two lines almost meet (weekend ÷ weekday ≈ 0.95–1.01). Late at night (00:00–03:00) weekends are 2–4 times busier than weekdays.

  
Overall, a weekday averages about 105,000 Manhattan-internal trips and a weekend day about 68,000. Riding on weekdays is concentrated into commute hours, while weekend riding is spread across the afternoon, which fits the hypothesis.

  
Reading the view. The chart compares how much people ride and when. A peak at 08:00 is consistent with commuting, but it is not direct evidence of trip purpose.
