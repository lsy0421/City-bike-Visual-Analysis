Departure hotspots across the day

Question of interest

How do departure hotspots change across morning, midday, and evening, and how do the patterns differ between ordinary weekdays and weekends?

Three-hour windows: morning [07:00, 10:00), midday [11:00, 14:00), evening [16:00, 19:00). Circle area shows the station's average departures per day within the window (divided by 21 or 8 days). All six maps share the same extent and symbol scale.

What the visualization shows

  
Weekday morning (about 20,100 departures per day in the window): the biggest circles are at transit hubs and job centers. Park Ave & E 42 St, next to Grand Central, is the busiest at 144.3 per day, followed by Pier 61 at Chelsea Piers (116.2), W 31 St & 7 Ave near Penn Station (109.9), W 21 St & 6 Ave and 9 Ave & W 33 St (about 106 each). These are rides away from rail terminals for the last part of a commute.

  
Weekday evening is the busiest panel (about 29,450 per day), and the same Midtown/Chelsea hubs grow larger: 9 Ave & W 33 St near Penn Station / Hudson Yards reaches 244 per day, W 21 St & 6 Ave 200, Park Ave & E 42 St 187. Weekday midday is much quieter (about 14,000) and spread out, with Central Park South and Columbus Circle (Broadway & W 58 St) appearing among the top stations.

  
Weekend morning is nearly empty (about 4,830 per day, a quarter of the weekday level), with no station above 32 per day. On weekend midday and evening (about 13,900 and 15,570 per day) the hotspots are park and waterfront stations: Pier 61 at Chelsea Piers on the Hudson River Greenway (114–129), Central Park S & 6 Ave (107–114), Central Park W & W 72 St and 10 Ave & W 14 St by the High Line. Weekend midday has about the same volume as weekday midday, but it is centered on the parks and the Hudson waterfront rather than Midtown streets.

  
Reading the view. This view shows where departures are concentrated. Q3 separately shows the balance between arrivals and departures. Each window is three hours long, so circle sizes can be compared across panels.
